## AI INDUSTRY

### Anthropic's draft IPO filing shows $16.3 billion in half-year revenue and more than $600 billion in compute commitments

The Wall Street Journal reported on October 20 that it had reviewed a preliminary S-1 from Anthropic showing $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, up from $1.1 billion a year earlier, against an $80.7 billion net loss - roughly $72 billion of it non-cash charges on convertible notes. The draft lists compute commitments above $600 billion, including non-cancelable contracts of $111 billion with Google, $110 billion with Amazon Web Services and $31 billion with Microsoft, and shows two customers supplying about 20% of revenue. Anthropic declined to comment; no filing has appeared with the SEC.

**In plain terms:** Anthropic makes Claude, an AI assistant businesses pay to use. Before selling shares publicly a company files a document called an S-1 laying out its finances; this is a draft a reporter was shown, not a filed one. Sales grew roughly fifteen-fold in a year, but the company has promised over $600 billion to rent computers from Google, Amazon and Microsoft, and most of that is owed whether or not it uses them. The enormous loss is mostly an accounting entry, not cash out the door.

**Why it matters:** Reuters reported an earlier version of the draft on September 28 with $518 billion in commitments, so the figure keeps growing - and it is the obligation, not the technology, that has to be refinanced every year.

## INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### Iran's Revolutionary Guard says its mines stopped three tankers in the Strait of Hormuz as Brent tops $110

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said mines halted three tankers it accused of using routes Tehran had not authorised through the Strait of Hormuz - two set ablaze and a third, carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude, exploding. Reuters, citing Kpler tracking data, counted at least 18 tanker attacks in two weeks. Brent traded near $110.38 a barrel early Wednesday after reaching $111.99 on Tuesday, a five-month high. Houthi forces separately claimed strikes on Saudi Aramco sites at Yanbu and Shaybah, which Saudi authorities have not confirmed. Tehran's terms for talks, set Tuesday: sanctions lifted, threats ended, and "not a gram" of enriched uranium leaving the country.

**In plain terms:** The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow stretch of sea at the mouth of the Persian Gulf that about a fifth of the world's oil travels through. Iran says it has mined parts of it and is deciding which ships may pass. Mines are cheap to lay and slow to clear, so a handful can make shipowners refuse a route no navy has swept. That is why oil is up: barrels have not stopped moving, but insurers and captains are charging for the chance that they will.

**Why it matters:** Mining a chokepoint is far harder to undo than a blockade - clearance takes months after any ceasefire, so this price shock will outlast whatever deal ends the shooting.

## ECONOMICS

### Australia's central bank says the oil shock has made its inflation outlook materially worse

Reserve Bank of Australia Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser told a Citi conference in Sydney on October 21 that the bank's inflation outlook is "materially worse" than its August forecast, citing higher oil prices, and said the board faces "a world of hard choices." Market-implied odds of an increase at the November 2-3 meeting moved from roughly one in four to about even. The cash rate stands at 4.60% after four increases this year. Hauser called September's rise in unemployment to 4.7% weaker than expected, while cautioning the figure may contain "noise."

**In plain terms:** A central bank sets the interest rate that feeds into mortgages and business loans; raising it cools spending and, eventually, prices. Oil is the awkward input, because it runs through almost everything that moves - food, freight, plastics - and no central bank can drill for more of it. So the choice is between tolerating higher inflation and deliberately slowing your own economy to offset a price rise caused abroad. Hauser's point was that neither option avoids damage.

**Why it matters:** Australia is the first developed-economy central bank to say on the record that the Hormuz shock has changed its rate path, which is the mechanism by which a blocked shipping lane turns into higher mortgage payments worldwide.

## HEALTHCARE

### FDA approves GSK's Jemperli as the first immunotherapy for a genetic subset of rectal cancer

The FDA granted accelerated approval on October 19 to GSK's Jemperli (dostarlimab) for adults with stage II or III locally advanced rectal cancer whose tumours are MSI-H or dMMR, the first immunotherapy cleared in that setting. The decision rests on AZUR-1, a single-arm phase 2 trial of 154 previously untreated patients, in which 68% of evaluable patients had a sustained clinical complete response at 12 months. Serious adverse reactions occurred in 20%. GSK had expected a decision in February 2027; the application carried priority review and a National Priority Voucher, and GSK must keep following patients to confirm lasting benefit.

**In plain terms:** Rectal cancer is normally treated with chemotherapy, radiation and surgery, which can leave a patient with a permanent colostomy. In roughly 5 to 10% of these tumours the cell's DNA-repair machinery is broken, which litters it with mutations and makes it unusually easy for the immune system to spot. This drug releases a brake on immune cells so they attack, and in most patients the tumour disappeared without an operation. "Accelerated approval" means the FDA acted on early data and can pull the drug if longer follow-up disappoints.

**Why it matters:** This is organ preservation rather than survival carrying an approval, and the four-month-early clearance shows the FDA's new priority voucher compressing review timelines in practice rather than on paper.

Sources: [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-dostarlimab-locally-advanced-rectal-cancer) · [GSK](https://us.gsk.com/en-us/media/press-releases/jemperli-dostarlimab-gxly-approved-in-us-as-first-and-only-immunotherapy-for-adults-with-msi-hdmmr-locally-advanced-rectal-cancer/) · [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-approves-dostarlimab-rectal-cancer-2026a10013c7)

## WILDCARD - INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### Ukraine says the Pentagon is holding Patriot interceptors that NATO allies already paid for

Ukrainian officials and military officers told the Kyiv Independent that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has not approved transfer of a large batch of Patriot interceptors, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the same of AIM-9X missiles for NASAMS launchers - all already funded by NATO members. Zelenskyy said Ukraine received no US early warning of Russian ballistic missile attacks on three occasions last week, and said on October 20, after meeting US envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo, that the feed had resumed. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the reports "inaccurate," said Trump ordered no change, and said Patriot stocks are short and needed elsewhere.

**In plain terms:** A Patriot interceptor is a missile that shoots down an incoming missile. Each costs millions and only so many are built a year. European governments already bought these ones for Ukraine, so the argument is not about money but about whether Washington releases hardware it physically controls. The separate issue is early warning: American satellites spot a Russian launch minutes before Ukrainian radar can, and those minutes are what gets people into shelters. Both sides agree deliveries slowed; they disagree on whether anyone chose to slow them.

**Why it matters:** Supplies that allies have already paid for are now a lever the executive branch can pull without a vote in Congress, which makes Ukraine's air defence a function of negotiating posture rather than of contracts.

## WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### Chinese tech giants order more than 3 million Nvidia workstation cards worth over $35 billion

Reuters reported that ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have together ordered more than 3 million Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 cards, priced near $11,400 each, for a combined list value above $35 billion. Total Chinese orders exceed 6 million cards, while Nvidia has told partners its global 2027 output of the export-compliant part will not exceed 3 million and that allocations may come to 5% or 10% of requests. The RTX PRO 5500 is a workstation GPU, less capable than Nvidia's H200 and B300; US rules bar Blackwell-based B300 sales to China and license H200 shipments case by case.

**In plain terms:** Export controls stop China buying Nvidia's fastest AI chips, so Chinese firms are buying the best thing they are allowed: a card built for one engineer's desktop, ordered by the million and wired together in racks. Per unit it is a worse chip, and stringing many weak ones together wastes electricity and bandwidth moving data between them. The striking part is the shortage: orders are roughly double what Nvidia can build next year, so most of this demand goes unmet whatever Washington decides.

**Why it matters:** When demand runs at twice available supply, the binding constraint on Chinese AI compute becomes Nvidia's factory capacity rather than US export policy - which means tightening the rules further buys little.

## WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### US sanctions five Chinese entities, including a venture firm, over stolen Google AI trade secrets

The State Department on October 19 designated five Chinese entities under the Protecting American Intellectual Property Act over the theft of Google AI trade secrets: Shanghai Zhisuan Technology, the venture firm MiraclePlus (Qiji Chuangtan Beijing Investment Management) and three affiliated funds and partnerships. Shanghai Zhisuan was founded in 2023 by Linwei Ding, a former Google engineer convicted by a federal jury on January 29 on seven counts of theft of trade secrets. State said MiraclePlus provided "significant financial, material, or technological support" for the theft and benefited from it, having seeded the company for equity. The designations freeze assets and bar transactions with US persons; Beijing has threatened retaliation.

**In plain terms:** Trade secrets are technical details a company deliberately never publishes - here, designs for the specialised hardware Google uses to train AI. An engineer was convicted of taking them, then founded a company in China built around them. What is new is that the US is punishing the investors who funded that company, not only the person who took the files. Sanctions cut a firm off from American banks and partners, which for a venture fund that raises and deploys dollars is close to fatal.

**Why it matters:** Extending sanctions to the capital behind a theft rather than the thief puts every cross-border AI investor on notice about diligence they have not previously had to perform.

## WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### Roivant's brepocitinib hits the primary endpoint in both phase 3 uveitis trials

Roivant and its Priovant subsidiary said on October 21 that the CLARITY-1 and CLARITY-2 phase 3 trials of brepocitinib in non-infectious uveitis both met their primary endpoint. Across 371 patients randomised to brepocitinib 45 mg once daily or placebo for 48 weeks, median time to treatment failure exceeded 48 weeks on the drug against about 12 weeks on placebo. Pooled, 62% on brepocitinib reached steroid-free quiescence at week 48 versus 20% on placebo, and 42% of eyes entering with macular oedema had it resolve, versus 4.8%. The companies reported no new safety signals and plan a supplemental new drug application in the first half of 2027.

**In plain terms:** Uveitis is inflammation inside the eye that destroys sight if it keeps flaring. The standard treatment is steroids, which work but cause bone loss, diabetes and cataracts over time, so the goal is keeping the eye calm without them. This pill blocks two signalling enzymes immune cells use to sustain inflammation. The number worth noting is the placebo arm: those patients failed in about twelve weeks, showing how fast the disease returns once steroids are tapered. These are the company's own figures, not yet reviewed by regulators.

**Why it matters:** A non-steroid pill that holds remission for a year would displace the long-term steroid regimens that cause much of the collateral damage in this disease, and the same enzyme-blocking approach is in trials for several other autoimmune conditions.

## TECH

### SpaceX posts its first profitable quarter as a public company and opens merger talks with Tesla

SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion on October 20, up 161% year over year and above the $12.72 billion consensus, with net income of $4.4 billion - its first profit since listing. Starlink, reported as Connectivity, rose 69% to $5.25 billion on 13.6 million subscribers, up from 12 million, and still supplies nearly all of the company's profit. The Super Intelligence segment formed by February's xAI merger booked $7.56 billion, up 830%, overtaking Starlink as the largest revenue line. Capital spending was $19.6 billion, with $35-40 billion guided for the fourth quarter. Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees of independent directors to discuss a merger.

**In plain terms:** Starlink is SpaceX's satellite internet service, sold mainly to households that cannot get a cable run to them. It has 13.6 million subscribers and is where essentially all the company's profit comes from. The quarter's growth headline came instead from the AI business SpaceX absorbed from xAI, which brings in more revenue but little profit. A "special committee of independent directors" is what a board sets up when a deal would benefit someone sitting on both sides of it - here Musk, who controls both companies.

**Why it matters:** Forming the committees is the procedural step before a deal, and a combined company would fold Tesla's cash flow into a capital programme already spending close to $40 billion a quarter.

## TRUMP NEWS

### Trump announced a 15-month golf course closure that his own lawyers told a federal judge did not exist

Trump posted on Truth Social that East Potomac Golf Links in Washington would close on November 15 for 15 months and that its redesign should begin "immediately." At a nearly seven-hour hearing on October 19, Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward told US District Judge Ana Reyes, "There is no plan to close the course," later qualifying that there was none "as those words have their literal meaning." National Park Service Acting Director Jessica Bowron testified that she first learned of the planned closure from Trump's post. Reyes asked from the bench: "I have, on the one hand, the president of the United States saying to the American public three times now... there is a final plan to redesign the golf course. Then I have you all telling me and... witnesses saying what they're saying, that there's no final plan. Which is it?" Trump had called Reyes a "crazed lunatic" that morning. She denied the Justice Department's recusal motion in a 13-page opinion, extended her order barring further tree removal through October 26, and said the government's conduct "has cost the government all of its credibility with me in this case." At least 430 trees have been cleared after the government told the court it planned to remove up to 529.

**In plain terms:** The president wants a public golf course in Washington rebuilt, and a lawsuit is trying to stop the trees being cut down for it. In court the government's position was that no final plan existed yet - the argument that stops a judge blocking something not yet decided. Then the president described the plan in public, with a closing date and a length. The park service official responsible for the course testified she found out the same way everyone else did.

**Why it matters:** The contradiction is now in a hearing transcript rather than a news cycle, which turns a messaging problem into a credibility finding the judge can cite in the ruling itself.
