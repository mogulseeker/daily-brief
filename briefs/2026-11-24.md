### Nvidia's board brings Jensen Huang back as chief executive twelve days after he resigned

Five days before bringing him back, the board told investors it did not intend to. The investigation has concluded nothing.

**Why it matters:** A board that reversed its own public position in five days has told investors Huang is not replaceable, which concentrates the risk in the entire AI trade on one person's standing.

## INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### US Treasury sanctions the money-changing network Iran uses to get paid for its oil

The Treasury Department on November 23 sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and individuals under what it called Operation Economic Outcast, targeting the “shadow banking” system it says the Central Bank of Iran oversees. FinCEN said it identified roughly $14.7bn of financial activity potentially linked to that network between January 2025 and August 2026. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called the target the regime's last financial lifeline. Iran's rial fell on the black market on Tuesday.

**In plain terms:** Sanctions cut Iran off from the ordinary banking system, so it sells oil and collects payment through chains of money changers and shell companies abroad. That is what “shadow banking” means here. Treasury seizes nothing: it makes it illegal for any bank that touches the US dollar to deal with the named firms, which locks them out. Watch the rial, which fell again on Tuesday. That is Iranians' savings losing value, not the state's.

**Why it matters:** The sanctions aim at the mechanism that has let Iran absorb a seven-month naval blockade, which shortens the time Tehran can hold out without a deal.

## ECONOMICS

### A December rate rise is now priced as near certain after the New York Fed president says the Fed must keep acting

John Williams, president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, said on November 23 that “we must take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2% goal,” while telling reporters he had no particular timing in mind and that “I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action.” He expects inflation to end 2026 at 3.5% and reach 2% only in 2028. Traders put the odds of a quarter-point rise on December 9 at about 98%, from roughly 88% on Friday; the two-year Treasury yield rose seven basis points to 4.69%.

**In plain terms:** The Fed raises interest rates to make borrowing more expensive, which slows spending and eventually price rises. Williams sits near the middle of the committee rather than at either edge, so traders read him as its direction rather than one person's view. Notice what he ruled out as well as what he urged: the half-point jump one governor has been pushing for. The market read is a quarter point in December and no promise beyond it.

**Why it matters:** The argument inside the Fed has moved from whether to raise rates to how fast, which prices a higher cost of credit into 2027 whatever December's exact number turns out to be.

## HEALTHCARE

### FDA clears the first new post-surgery hormone treatment for early breast cancer in more than two decades

The FDA approved Roche's Wilserdly (giredestrant) on November 23 as a single-agent adjuvant treatment for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative stage I-III breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence. In the phase 3 lidERA trial, the once-daily pill cut the relative risk of invasive recurrence or death by 30% against standard endocrine therapy. It is the first selective estrogen receptor degrader approved for early-stage ER-positive disease and, by Roche's account, the first new endocrine therapy cleared in that setting in over 20 years.

**In plain terms:** About two in three breast cancers grow in response to estrogen, so the standard treatment after surgery is years of pills that block the hormone's effect. This drug works differently: instead of blocking the receptor the hormone lands on, it degrades the receptor itself. That matters because tumours often escape the older drugs by mutating that receptor. Read the result precisely: the trial found 30% fewer recurrences, not 30% fewer deaths.

**Why it matters:** Adjuvant treatment reaches a far larger group of patients than advanced disease does, so clearance in the early-stage setting turns a specialist drug into a years-long default prescription.

### Amazon buys the software that makes its own AI chips run faster, and the team that wrote it

Amazon said on November 23 it had signed an agreement to acquire Decart's AI optimization technology and bring the Israeli startup's co-founders and research team into Annapurna Labs, its in-house chip unit. Bloomberg reported the price at about $7.5bn; Amazon has not confirmed a figure, and it is not buying Decart's AI models. The software raises the throughput of existing silicon, including Amazon's Trainium and Inferentia chips as well as Nvidia's. **In plain terms:** Training an AI model comes down to how fast you can push numbers through a chip, and much of that speed comes from software that schedules the work well rather than from the chip itself. Amazon designs its own chips and wants to sell them against Nvidia's, whose advantage is that years of software has been written for its hardware. Buying the team that writes that layer is faster than waiting for outsiders to.

**Why it matters:** Amazon is buying the software layer that makes a chip usable at all, which is the real barrier facing anyone trying to sell an alternative to Nvidia.

## WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### AP finds the DEA let 1.4 million fentanyl pills reach the street during its largest fentanyl case

The Associated Press reported on November 24 that the Drug Enforcement Administration allowed at least 1.4 million fentanyl pills to go unseized during the New Mexico investigation that produced the largest fentanyl bust in US history, mostly between August 2024 and April 2025. The AP built the count from thousands of pages of court and investigative records, wiretap summaries and 12 hours of DEA surveillance footage. The DEA has denied knowingly allowing trafficking and declined to answer questions, citing a Justice Department inspector general review. New Mexico's attorney general has opened a criminal investigation.

**In plain terms:** Narcotics agents sometimes watch a shipment move instead of seizing it, hoping to follow it upward and arrest whoever runs the operation. The cost is that the drugs reach buyers meanwhile. The Justice Department told agents in 2017 to seize fentanyl whenever practicable, because the amount that kills is so small; those instructions were loosened in 2024. What the AP did was count what moved while agents watched.

**Why it matters:** A criminal investigation and an inspector general review now turn on a tactic used routinely across drug enforcement, not on the conduct of one squad.

## WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### Virginia and Oregon become the first states to sue over images Grok generated of children

The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon sued SpaceX separately on November 23 over its xAI business, alleging it failed to prevent Grok from generating sexual images of real people without their consent, including child sexual abuse material, and represented the products as generally safe. Virginia sued under its Consumer Protection Act and Oregon under its Unlawful Trade Practices Act; both seek injunctions, civil penalties and disgorgement. Virginia cites a Center for Countering Digital Hate estimate that more than 20,000 of the sexualized images Grok produced on X appear to depict children.

**In plain terms:** Grok is the AI built into X that makes pictures on request. The states are not arguing that AI-made pictures are themselves illegal. They are using consumer-protection law, which says a company cannot market something as safe when it is not, and that is a lower bar than proving the company made the images. Nothing is decided: these are complaints and no court has ruled.

**Why it matters:** Consumer-protection law lets state attorneys general attack an AI product's safety claims without arguing about Section 230 or copyright, which is the template other states can copy.

## TECH

### CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for about $44bn in the largest cybersecurity deal attempted

CrowdStrike announced on November 23 an all-stock agreement to acquire Zscaler at a value of about $43.9bn, exchanging 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share and leaving CrowdStrike holders with roughly 84.1% of the combined company. George Kurtz remains chief executive; Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry will run the Zscaler business and join the board. The companies say the deal moves CrowdStrike into network security and expands their addressable market by more than $185bn. CrowdStrike shares fell roughly 9%. Closing is expected in about a year.

**In plain terms:** CrowdStrike protects the laptops and servers inside a company. Zscaler sits in front of the internet connection and decides which traffic gets through. Large customers have been buying both and complaining about running two systems, so the pitch is one vendor, one console, one bill. Investors sold CrowdStrike because paying in shares means issuing a great many new ones, shrinking the slice each existing owner holds.

**Why it matters:** It tests whether regulators will allow the two dominant layers of enterprise security to combine, and the answer sets the ceiling for consolidation across the industry.

**Why it matters:** Whether the assurances Trump describes rested on any security review is now a question for a Senate committee, and the answer is on the record in nine days.
