## HEALTHCARE

### Kymera's once-daily eczema pill matched injectable biologics in a mid-stage trial

Kymera Therapeutics reported this morning that all three doses of KT-621, an oral STAT6 degrader, met every primary and secondary endpoint in BROADEN2, a Phase 2b trial in 244 adults and adolescents with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis. The mid dose cut Eczema Area and Severity Index scores by a mean 72% at week 16 against 27% on placebo; 62% reached EASI-75 versus 22%, and itch scores fell 58% against 21%. Conjunctivitis occurred in 11.1% to 19.7% of treated patients, all mild to moderate with no discontinuations. Phase 3 is planned by mid-2027, and shares rose about 28% premarket on company topline data that has not been peer-reviewed.

**In plain terms:** Severe eczema is treated mainly with biologics, antibody drugs that must be injected and cost a great deal. KT-621 is a pill that destroys STAT6, a protein inside cells that relays the inflammation signal driving the disease. A pill matching injection-level skin clearance is what oral eczema drugs have repeatedly failed to do. The catch is in the safety table: up to one in five patients got pink eye, and this is company data awaiting a larger trial.

**Why it matters:** If a pill holds up at this level through Phase 3, the economics of treating inflammatory disease change, because the barrier to starting treatment stops being an injection and a specialty pharmacy.

## WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### FDA testing found hormones in toothpaste and mouthwash, half of the recalled products marketed for children

ORL Labs LLC of Scottsdale, Arizona is recalling 51,762 units of six ORL Organic Oral Care products, three toothpastes and three mouthwashes, three of them marketed for children, after FDA sampling detected progesterone, estradiol or dehydroepiandrosterone in samples. None is declared on the label. The company attributes it to manufacturing cross-contamination and began the recall on October 12; the FDA classified it Class II on November 6, meaning temporary or medically reversible harm with serious harm remote. No illnesses have been linked. The recall reached national coverage over the weekend through FDA enforcement reporting.

**In plain terms:** Progesterone, estradiol and DHEA are sex hormones, the same molecules used in hormone therapy and birth control. Finding them in toothpaste means residue from something made on the same equipment, a hygiene failure rather than a chosen ingredient. Hormones matter more in children at the same dose, which is why the three kids' products are the alarming part. Class II is the FDA's middle tier: real risk, probably reversible, nobody reported hurt.

**Why it matters:** The volume is small but the failure mode is not: hormone residue in a children's product points at a contract manufacturer running incompatible product lines on shared equipment.

## TECH

### A new Commerce rule closed the loophole letting Chinese chip designers buy advanced manufacturing abroad

A Bureau of Industry and Security revision published November 16 and effective around November 19 changed how foundries may use transistor counts to certify a chip falls outside US export controls: the test now also depends on where the chip was designed and where the designer's ultimate parent is headquartered, so a Chinese-owned design house operating through a Singapore office no longer counts as Singaporean. Reuters, citing two people and TSMC guidance it reviewed, reported TSMC has told Chinese AI chip designers to stop ordering 16-nanometre and more advanced processes without a US licence, leaving them 28nm and 40nm provided the chips avoid high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging. The Financial Times reported Samsung has also suspended some shipments pending licences.

**In plain terms:** China cannot yet manufacture the most advanced chips, so Chinese companies design them at home and send the designs to TSMC in Taiwan to be built. The old rule let the factory decide by counting transistors, a measurement a design could be waved through on. Now the factory must also check who designed it and who owns them, so nationality decides and a Singapore subsidiary no longer helps. Chinese AI projects drop to roughly decade-old process technology.

**Why it matters:** Export control has shifted from policing what a chip is to policing who the customer ultimately belongs to, which is far harder to engineer around and far easier for Washington to enforce.

### A second judge blocked the triumphal arch, rejecting the claim that a 1925 statute authorized it

Judge Leonie Brinkema of the Eastern District of Virginia issued a preliminary injunction on Friday, November 20 indefinitely halting construction of the proposed 250-foot triumphal arch at Arlington Memorial Circle, in a suit brought by Arlington County. She held the project lacks congressional approval, which the administration has not sought, said the government's argument that a 1925 statute already authorized it made no sense, and found the environmental review failed to adequately explain the arch's effect on nearby Reagan National Airport. The administration filed a notice of appeal over the weekend; Brinkema is the second judge to block it, after Judge Tanya Chutkan's restraining order, extended into early December. PBS reports estimates above $100 million, funded through donations to the Trust for the National Mall that almost no donors have disclosed.

**In plain terms:** A triumphal arch is the monument Roman emperors built to commemorate their own victories. This one would stand 250 feet at the circle on the Virginia side of Memorial Bridge, paid for by donors whose names and amounts are mostly unknown. The legal problem is old and simple: Congress controls federal building on that land and was never asked. The ordinary detail that may decide it is the airport sitting beside the proposed structure.

**Why it matters:** Two judges have now found the arch legally unauthorized rather than merely procedurally flawed, which means the appeal has to win on the 1925 statute that one judge said made no sense.
