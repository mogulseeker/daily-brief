## 1. AI INDUSTRY

### Anthropic commits another $35 billion to rented computing power

Anthropic signed a $35 billion cloud-computing agreement with Lambda, an Nvidia-backed provider, for capacity at Hut 8's Beacon Point campus in Nueces County, Texas, Bloomberg and Reuters reported on August 31. Nvidia holds the lease on the roughly 350-megawatt site, Lambda installs the chips, and Anthropic buys the output for its Claude models. The deal follows this year's agreements of about $45 billion with Nscale, $50 billion with Fluidstack and $45 billion with SpaceX, taking Anthropic's disclosed 2026 compute commitments past $200 billion.

**In plain terms:** Anthropic is the company that makes Claude, and running a model like that means renting enormous amounts of specialised computer hardware in warehouse-sized buildings. This deal stacks four companies on top of each other: Hut 8, a former bitcoin miner, owns the Texas site and its electricity supply; Nvidia, which makes the chips, leases the building; Lambda fills it with those chips; Anthropic pays for the computing that comes out. The number to hold onto is the $200 billion of commitments this year, which is far more than the company currently earns. That is a bet that demand for AI keeps rising fast enough to cover it.

**Why it matters:** The chipmaker is now landlord, investor and supplier in the same deal, which means Nvidia's revenue increasingly depends on contracts Nvidia itself helped arrange.

### Two oil tankers hit by projectiles at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz

The Saudi-operated supertanker Sidr and South Korea's Senegal Prosperity were struck by unidentified projectiles within minutes of each other late on August 31 near Khasab, Oman, Bloomberg and UPI reported. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said one vessel took three projectiles about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab while completing an outbound transit; no casualties or spills were reported. The strikes followed US attacks on Iranian rocket launchers at Larak Island on August 30 and Iranian retaliation against US bases in Jordan.

**In plain terms:** The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow sea passage at the mouth of the Persian Gulf that about a fifth of the world's oil normally travels through. Six months into the war between the US and Iran, shipping there has largely collapsed, and the tankers still making the run are being shot at. Nobody has claimed these two attacks, and the maritime agency that logged them describes the projectiles only as "unknown," so blame is not established. What is established is the pattern: American strikes, Iranian counterstrikes on US bases, and commercial ships caught in between.

**Why it matters:** Each unclaimed attack raises the insurance cost of every remaining transit, which tightens oil supply without either government having to declare an escalation.

### The House sends Trump a stopgap bill, moving the next shutdown fight past the midterms

The House voted 370-48 on September 1 to extend federal funding through December 11 and sent the measure to the president, who is expected to sign it; the Senate passed the same text on August 8. The bill temporarily blocks an Office of Management and Budget rule that would route federal grant awards through political appointees for review, and bars the administration from moving money out of other programs into Border Patrol accounts.

**In plain terms:** Congress has to pass spending bills or agencies run out of money and shut down. Rather than fight over the details now, both parties agreed to keep spending at current levels until December 11, which lands the next confrontation after November's midterm elections. The vote was lopsided, 370-48, because neither party wanted a shutdown weeks before voters decide who controls Congress. Two conditions got attached: the White House budget office cannot make political appointees vet grant money, and it cannot shift funds from other programmes into border enforcement.

**Why it matters:** Both riders target the administration's practice of redirecting money Congress already appropriated, which is the fight the December deadline will actually be about.

## 4. HEALTHCARE

### Novartis halts eight cell-therapy trials after three deaths, and Bristol Myers pauses its own

Novartis stopped enrollment in eight trials of rapcabtagene autoleucel, an experimental CAR-T therapy for autoimmune and neurological disease, after three patients died of complications from immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome, The Wall Street Journal reported August 31. The paused studies cover lupus, myasthenia gravis, multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis and vasculitis. Bristol Myers Squibb separately paused enrollment across its zola-cel autoimmune programme after what it called "transient and reversible inflammatory events," with no deaths reported.

**In plain terms:** CAR-T therapy takes a patient's own immune cells, re-engineers them to attack a specific target, and puts them back. It works well in some blood cancers, and both companies were testing whether it could reset the immune system in diseases where the body attacks itself. The syndrome that killed the three Novartis patients is the immune system overreacting so violently that it damages the patient's own organs. The detail researchers are focused on is that both companies use fast manufacturing processes to make these cells, which may be what the two safety signals have in common. Bristol Myers has reported no deaths, so the two situations are not equivalent.

**Why it matters:** Autoimmune CAR-T was the field's main plan for expanding cell therapy beyond cancer into diseases affecting millions, and it now has a safety question attached to the manufacturing method itself.

## 6. WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### The EU designates ChatGPT a very large online search engine, its strictest platform category

The European Commission on August 31 designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act, the first standalone AI service brought under the bloc's toughest platform rules. The trigger was ChatGPT's web-search function, which averaged about 159 million monthly active users in the EU over the six months to March 2026, far above the 45 million threshold. OpenAI has four months, to January 2027, to comply. Reddit and Roblox were designated the same day.

**In plain terms:** The Digital Services Act is the EU's law for large online platforms, and crossing 45 million monthly users in the bloc moves a service into its heaviest tier of obligations: annual assessments of the risks the service creates, a public archive of every advert it runs, data access for outside researchers, transparency reports twice a year, and a procedure for emergencies. The classification does not rest on ChatGPT being an AI product. It rests on the fact that ChatGPT searches the live web and shows people results, which under this law makes it a search engine.

**Why it matters:** Regulators are reaching AI systems through rules written for search and social media, which is faster than waiting for the EU's dedicated AI Act obligations to bite.

### Bessent tells G20 finance ministers that allies back the campaign to cut off Iran's economy

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US has allied support for its effort to "economically asphyxiate" Iran, speaking as G20 finance leaders met in the Blue Ridge Mountains, The Washington Post reported September 1. The campaign, announced August 24, targets five sectors Iran uses to raise revenue - digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping - and has so far sanctioned nearly 60 entities, individuals and vessels. Bessent said then: "Our objective is to sever every economic lifeline that sustains this tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone."

**In plain terms:** Sanctions only work if other countries enforce them, because a bank or shipping firm that ignores Washington can keep trading with Iran unless its own government stops it. That is what this meeting was for: getting the finance ministers of the largest economies to agree to police the same restrictions. The five sectors named are the workarounds Iran has used before, including cryptocurrency and gold, which are harder to trace than bank transfers. Bessent says allies are on board. The Post is reporting his characterisation of those conversations, not a signed agreement, so treat the level of support as his claim.

**Why it matters:** Squeezing Iran's revenue while its forces are attacking shipping in the Gulf gives Tehran less to lose from further escalation, not more.

### Sony and Warner's publishing arms sue Anthropic, naming its founders personally

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell Music and affiliated publishers filed a 48-page complaint against Anthropic, chief executive Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann, alleging the company built Claude using song lyrics obtained from pirate libraries including Library Genesis and scraped from licensed lyric sites such as Musixmatch and LyricFind. The suit covers tens of thousands of works, including "I Am the Walrus" and "Hallelujah," and seeks up to $150,000 per infringed song. Anthropic said it will "defend ourselves robustly."

**In plain terms:** Music publishers own the words and music of songs, separately from the recordings. They say Anthropic downloaded lyrics from sites that had no right to give them away, used them to train Claude, and stripped out the copyright notices along the way. The unusual part is that the complaint names Amodei and Mann as individual defendants rather than only the company, which if it survives would put executives personally on the hook. Anthropic's public response notes this is the third suit from the same lawyers on overlapping claims. Nothing has been proven; these are allegations in a filing.

**Why it matters:** Naming founders personally is an attempt to make the cost of training on scraped data land on the people who decide to do it, not just on the corporate balance sheet.

## 10. TRUMP NEWS

### The US Mint begins selling dollar coins bearing the sitting president's portrait

At noon Eastern today the US Mint opens sales of rolls and bags of the 2026 Semiquincentennial President Donald J. Trump $1 coin: $61 for a roll of 25 coins with $25 of face value, and $154.50 for a bag of 100. The obverse carries Trump's portrait with the inscriptions "LIBERTY," "IN GOD WE TRUST" and "1776~2026." The authorising statute, the Circulating Collectible Coin Redesign Act of 2020, states that no portrait of a living person may appear on the reverse of these coins. The draft design released in October 2025 placed Trump on the reverse, raising a fist above the words "FIGHT FIGHT FIGHT." The final coin moves his portrait to the obverse and puts the Presidential Seal on the reverse.

**In plain terms:** A coin has two sides: the obverse, or "heads," and the reverse, or "tails." The 2020 law that authorised a special dollar for America's 250th birthday says a living person's portrait cannot go on the tails side. It does not say the same about heads. The first published design put Trump on the tails side, which is the side the law restricts; the version now going on sale has him on heads instead, which the Treasury says is permitted. NPR reported in July that legal experts disagree and consider the coin unlawful, and Representative Ritchie Torres introduced a bill to bar living presidents from US money, which has not advanced. No court has ruled on the finished design.

**Why it matters:** The prohibition on living presidents on money was written to keep the currency from looking like it belongs to whoever currently holds office, and the government has now read it as a rule about which side of the coin.
