## 1. AI INDUSTRY

### Anthropic says Claude designed proteins that worked in the lab

Anthropic published a case study on August 19 reporting that its Claude models ran autonomous protein-design campaigns and produced 354 confirmed binders from 1,320 designs, succeeding against 14 of 15 targets. Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience built and tested the designs independently. Hit rates were 22.6% for Opus 4.8 and 26.7% for an unreleased model Anthropic calls Mythos Preview, rising to 35.1% in focused 24-hour sessions against an industry norm of 10-15%. On one target, RBX1, Claude hit 40% against 3.7% for entrants in Adaptyv's human design competition.

**In plain terms:** A protein binder is a custom-built protein designed to latch onto one specific target protein, and it is the starting point for a lot of modern drugs and lab tests. Designing one that actually sticks is hard; the field's normal success rate is roughly one in eight. Claude ran the design job on its own, and two outside companies then made the proteins physically and measured whether they stuck. About one in four worked. Anthropic states the catch itself: a binder is not a drug, and the same capability could help build dangerous biology, which is why it stays restricted rather than open.

**Why it matters:** If a model beats human specialists on a wet-lab-verified design task, the bottleneck in early drug discovery moves from ideas to lab capacity, and biosecurity rules written on the assumption that design is the hard part stop describing reality.

## 4. HEALTHCARE

### FDA approves a second drug for a rare disease that turns muscle into bone

The FDA approved Regeneron's Pasatru (garetosmab-grts) on August 19 for adults with fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, an ultra-rare genetic disorder in which muscle, tendon and ligament tissue turns into bone. It is the second FOP treatment the agency has cleared and the first shown to reduce clinician-assessed flare-ups in a placebo-controlled trial. Approval rested on the Phase 3 OPTIMA trial, which reported a 90-94% reduction in new heterotopic ossification lesions at 56 weeks. The antibody blocks Activin A, a protein Regeneron scientists identified as driving the lesions.

**In plain terms:** In FOP the body's repair system misfires and builds bone where bone should not be, inside muscles and connective tissue. Over years a person is gradually locked in place by a second skeleton, and surgery makes it worse, because cutting the tissue triggers more bone. Regeneron's researchers traced the misfire to a signaling protein called Activin A and built an antibody that blocks it. In the trial, patients formed roughly a tenth as many new bone lesions as before. It does not remove bone already formed, so it slows the disease rather than reversing it.

**Why it matters:** The approval validates a disease target Regeneron's own labs discovered, which is the uncommon case of a drug coming from a company's basic research rather than from licensing someone else's.

## 5. WILDCARD - ECONOMICS

### US federal debt passes $40 trillion

Treasury's daily statement put total federal debt at $40.05 trillion on August 18, a first, reported August 19. That is more than double the 2017 level, when the debt crossed $20 trillion; it passed $30 trillion four years ago. Interest now costs more than $1 trillion a year. The Washington Post reported the milestone arrived months early, partly because of revenue lost when the Supreme Court invalidated Trump's tariffs. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.7% on August 20 and the 30-year to 5.25%, near 20-month highs.

**In plain terms:** Federal debt is the running total of everything Washington has borrowed and not repaid, financed by selling bonds to investors. The yield is what those investors demand in return. When the yield rises, the government's own interest bill rises with it, which widens the deficit, which requires more borrowing. That loop is why the $1 trillion interest line matters more than the $40 trillion headline. The reason the milestone arrived early is unusual: tariff revenue the budget had counted on vanished when courts struck the tariffs down.

**Why it matters:** Interest is now a bigger annual cost than most federal programs, and the size of it is set by bond investors rather than by Congress.

A life sentence for a business collapse is unusual anywhere; the court found deliberate falsification of the accounts rather than only bad luck.

**Why it matters:** China is closing the accountability chapter of its property crisis without closing the financial one: the unfinished apartments and unpaid suppliers are still there.

**In plain terms:** Elimination does not mean zero cases. It means the virus is not spreading continuously inside the country, so every outbreak traces back to someone who brought it in from abroad. **Why it matters:** The elimination label is a scoreboard entry, but the coverage decline behind it is what sets the size of next year's outbreaks.

## 9. TECH

### Five US agencies warn that AI-written code is probing industrial plant controllers

The NSA, CISA, FBI, Department of Energy and EPA issued a joint advisory, CISA AA26-231A dated August 19, on active attacks against internet-exposed Siemens S7 series programmable logic controllers. The agencies said attackers pair open-source automation libraries, snap7.dll and python-snap7, with AI-assisted scripting to build tools that imitate legitimate monitoring software and read and write PLC memory, configuration data and ladder logic. Named sectors include energy, water and wastewater, chemical, and critical manufacturing. The advisory states: "This is not a theoretical risk - it is an active threat."

**In plain terms:** A programmable logic controller is the small industrial computer that physically runs a pump, a valve or a conveyor line. Many were designed decades ago on the assumption nobody untrusted could reach them, so they authenticate weakly, and a lot of them are in fact reachable from the open internet. The new element is not a novel exploit. Writing custom tooling for these obscure industrial protocols used to require a specialist, and AI assistance removes that barrier. The agencies say the intruders are so far only reading, which they read as mapping targets in advance.

**Why it matters:** This is the first time CISA has publicly named an AI-assisted campaign against operational technology, which moves "AI lowers the attacker skill floor" from forecast to documented incident.

### Trump tells the Supreme Court his ballroom is a national security facility

The injunction halting above-ground construction of Trump's White House ballroom takes effect today. Judge Richard Leon ruled the president cannot rebuild the White House without Congress; a divided DC Circuit panel upheld that on August 7. Solicitor General D. John Sauer filed an emergency application on August 14 describing the site as an "integrated military complex, including a totally secure ballroom space," "vitally required by national security" and "beyond the point of no return," citing a missile threat to Air Force One. Chief Justice Roberts set a Tuesday response deadline; as of early Friday the Court had not ruled. On July 31, 2025, Trump said of the project: "It won't interfere with the current building... It'll be near it, but not touching it, and pays total respect to the existing building." The East Wing, built in 1902, was demolished in full that fall. The stated cost moved from $200 million to $400 million; the Washington Post reported on June 16, 2026 that internal records showed a $600 million estimate with about half from taxpayers, against Trump's repeated statement that no taxpayer money is involved.

**In plain terms:** The dispute is about who controls the White House. It is federal property, and Congress decides what gets built on federal property; the president lives there but does not own it. Trump had the East Wing torn down and started a 90,000-square-foot ballroom without congressional approval, and a court ordered him to stop. His lawyers' answer is that the structure is really part of a secure military complex that happens to include a ballroom. Courts usually defer to the government on national-security claims, which is what makes that framing consequential.

**Why it matters:** If the label "national security facility" exempts a construction project from congressional control over federal property, the label is then available for the next project too.
