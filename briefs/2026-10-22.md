## 3. ECONOMICS

### UK inflation jumps to 3.5% as the oil shock reaches the pump

UK consumer price inflation rose to 3.5% in the year to September from 3.1% in August, the Office for National Statistics reported on October 21, above the 3.4% consensus and the fastest in about a year. Motor fuel drove it, up 29.8% year on year against 23.0% in August, but core inflation also climbed to 2.8% from 2.6% and services inflation to 3.7% from 3.4%. Bank Rate stands at 3.75%.

**In plain terms:** Inflation is the speed at which prices rise. British prices in September were 3.5% higher than a year earlier, up from 3.1%. Most of that jump traces to petrol and diesel, which cost nearly 30% more because fighting around the Strait of Hormuz has pushed up crude oil. The awkward part for the Bank of England is that its main tool, the interest rate, does nothing to oil prices; it works by making borrowing expensive enough to cool the whole economy.

**Why it matters:** Core inflation rose too, which is the signal that an energy shock is leaking into wages and services rather than passing straight through.

## 4. HEALTHCARE

### Nigeria confirms its first Ebola case and Africa CDC calls an emergency summit

Nigeria confirmed its first case in the current Ebola outbreak in Abuja on Tuesday, October 20, in a woman who arrived from Uganda on October 5 and was initially treated for malaria. Officials are tracing 44 contacts, most of them health workers. Africa CDC Director-General Jean Kaseya called an extraordinary virtual meeting of health ministers for October 28 to "strengthen our collective response." Nigeria is the fourth country hit by the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak that began in Congo in May.

**In plain terms:** Ebola spreads through contact with an infected person's blood or other bodily fluids and kills a large share of those who catch it. An outbreak that started in Congo in May has reached a fourth country, in a traveller who was first treated for malaria, because early symptoms of both are fever, aches and weakness. That delay is where the danger sits: most of the 44 people now monitored are the health workers who treated her beforehand. Uganda denies the infection originated there.

**Why it matters:** Abuja is a capital of roughly four million people with a major international airport, which turns a largely rural outbreak into an exportable one.

## 7. WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### Median launch price for a new US drug reached $257,000

The median annual net launch price for new US drugs was about $257,000 in 2025, up 35% in inflation-adjusted terms from $190,000 in 2022, according to an Institute for Clinical and Economic Review and West Health report dated October 20. About one in four 2025 launches exceeded $500,000 a year. Of the drugs ICER had reviewed, only about one in three were priced at or below its cost-effectiveness benchmark, and only about a third of outpatient prescriptions were filled within 30 days.

**In plain terms:** When a drug company launches a medicine it picks a price. ICER is an independent group that calculates what a medicine is worth based on how much good it does patients. Its report says the typical new American drug now launches at about $257,000 a year after discounts, a third more than three years ago once inflation is stripped out. Two findings sit oddly together: two-thirds cost more than ICER thinks they are worth, and only a third of prescriptions get filled within a month, mostly because insurers will not cover them.

**Why it matters:** The administration's price-cutting deals have targeted existing blockbusters, while the cost of everything newly approved kept climbing underneath them.

### Google Cloud starts selling OpenAI's models

OpenAI's frontier models and its Codex agentic coding workflows are being added to Google Cloud's Model Garden, the companies said on October 21, in private preview for selected customers with general availability planned in the coming weeks. Customers can buy through the Google Cloud Marketplace and apply existing Google Cloud spending commitments, making Google the only hyperscale cloud offering Gemini, Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's GPT models together. Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner.

**In plain terms:** Google and OpenAI build competing AI models, Gemini and GPT. Google's cloud division rents computing power to businesses, and it has now agreed to sell its rival's models to those customers alongside its own. The reason is procurement, not friendship: a company that already committed to spend tens of millions with Google would rather use that budget than negotiate a separate contract. By stocking every major model, Google makes itself the shop rather than one product on the shelf. Specific model names come from aggregators, not the companies.

**Why it matters:** Frontier models are being handled as interchangeable inventory, which moves power from whoever trains the best model toward whoever controls the sales channel.

## 9. TECH

### Lam Research posts record margins and falls 6% on a parts shortage

Lam Research reported adjusted earnings of $2.20 a share on revenue of $8.22 billion for its September quarter on October 21, ahead of consensus near $2.18 and $8.13 billion, with a record 52.4% gross margin and its first operating margin above 40%. Shares fell roughly 6% after hours anyway, after the chief financial officer said December-quarter guidance of $8.60 billion "would have been a bit higher" without component shortages that pushed shipments into the March quarter.

**In plain terms:** Lam Research makes the machines that chip factories use to manufacture chips, selling to firms like TSMC and Samsung rather than to consumers. It reported its most profitable quarter ever and the stock still dropped about 6%, because of one admission from its finance chief: it could have sold more, but could not get the parts to build its own machines. That pushes the bottleneck a step further back: the world is short of AI chips, factories are short of the equipment that makes them, and equipment makers are short of components.

**Why it matters:** When the binding constraint is components rather than demand, extra AI spending turns into a longer queue instead of more chips.

### Justice Department watchdog declines to investigate the prosecution his own department abandoned

Inspector General Don Berthiaume declined, in an October 8 letter, to investigate the failed felony prosecution of former Olympic canoeist David Hearn over damage to the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, writing that statutory limits required him to refer prosecutorial-misconduct allegations to the Office of Professional Responsibility, whose head the attorney general appoints. The case collapsed in late July when US Attorney Jeanine Pirro's office told the court the damage was "the result of flawed installation by the contractor, Atlantic Industrial Coatings"; Judge Todd Edelman, dismissing it, wrote that "the government shot first and asked questions later." Trump posted on August 1 that he disagreed with his own department: "To me, it was a pure case of VANDALISM."

**In plain terms:** Prosecutors charged a former Olympic athlete with a felony for wrecking the Reflecting Pool at the Lincoln Memorial. Then the Justice Department's own lawyers looked harder, told the court the damage came from a contractor's bad installation work, and asked for the charges to be dropped. The judge threw the case out and wrote that the government had charged first and checked afterwards. The president publicly disagreed with his own department and kept calling it vandalism. Now the department's independent watchdog says the law does not let him examine how the case was brought.

**Why it matters:** The referral moves the question of how those charges were brought from an independent watchdog to an office inside the chain of command it would have to examine.
