## 1. AI INDUSTRY

### The FTC has subpoenaed OpenAI and Anthropic over safety incidents

The Federal Trade Commission has sent civil investigative demands of more than 50 pages each to OpenAI and Anthropic, seeking records on security incidents, internal safeguarding practices, marketing materials and privacy policies. The FTC confirmed to CBS News that it issued them but declined to describe the scope. Reporting, originating with Axios citing a person familiar with the matter, says the agency also intends to compel testimony from AI executives and the evaluation group METR. OpenAI said it will "engage constructively with the FTC." Accounts differ on whether the demands went out October 14 or 15; this is an investigation, not a finding of wrongdoing.

**In plain terms:** The Federal Trade Commission polices companies for lying to customers. A civil investigative demand is its version of a subpoena: a legal order to hand over documents. What it asked for is the interesting part. Instead of asking whether the chatbots give bad answers, it asked for security-incident records and the marketing copy side by side, which sets up a comparison between what these companies promised and what they knew internally. That is far easier to prove than harm.

**Why it matters:** A consumer-protection document demand is how a regulator with no AI statute still gets to read a frontier lab's internal incident reports, and those documents outlive the investigation.

## 2. INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### A second cargo ship has sunk in the Strait of Hormuz, and Washington and Tehran disagree on what hit it

The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank in the Strait of Hormuz after a strike set it ablaze; all 21 crew escaped unhurt, and Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority attributed the damage to "unknown projectiles." Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed a naval mine, while US Central Command said an IRGC projectile hit it and security firm Marisks found no independent evidence of a mine. Its sister ship GFS Giselle was struck separately and another tanker caught fire Thursday. CENTCOM said over 40 commercial ships transited in 24 hours, disputing Iran's claim the strait is shut. Brent traded near $105, against roughly $72 before the war began February 28.

**In plain terms:** The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow neck of sea that much of the world's oil must pass through. Whether it is open is now a factual dispute: Iran says it closed it, US Central Command counts 40 ships through in a day. The argument over this one ship follows the same logic. A mine is left behind and shuts a lane by making every captain afraid; a missile is aimed. An independent firm can find evidence of neither.

**Why it matters:** Insurers price the fear rather than the barrels, so an unresolved question about whether mines are in the water raises the cost of every cargo through Hormuz regardless of how many ships get through.

## 3. ECONOMICS

### Wholesale prices posted their biggest monthly jump since early 2022

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday that the producer price index for final demand rose 1.3% in September, the largest monthly advance since a 1.7% rise in March 2022, and 6.3% over 12 months unadjusted, the most since December 2022. More than 80% of the increase came from final demand energy, up 10.8%, with crude petroleum up 28.3% in the month. Excluding food, energy and trade services, prices still rose 0.5% after 0.3% in August, and 5.0% over 12 months. Consumer inflation had printed at 3.7% a day earlier.

**In plain terms:** The producer price index measures what producers get paid, one step upstream of store shelves, so a jump here often reaches consumers a month or two later. Almost all of this one is the Iran war arriving in the data, with crude oil up 28.3% in a single month. The number to watch is the other one: strip out food, energy and trade and prices still rose 0.5%. The increase is no longer confined to the pump.

**Why it matters:** An oil shock that stays in energy is something the Fed can look through; a 5.0% core rate says it is spreading, which is why a rate cut is no longer the market's base case.

## 4. HEALTHCARE

### Medicare enrollment opened with 3.2 million people needing a new plan

Medicare's annual enrollment window opened Thursday and runs through December 7. About 3.2 million people in individual Medicare Advantage plans, roughly 11% of that market, must pick new coverage for 2027 because their plans are being discontinued, per a Medicare Market Insights analysis of federal data; UnitedHealth and Humana are among the insurers withdrawing. KFF estimates 638,000 beneficiaries in nearly 200 counties will have no Medicare Advantage plan at all in 2027, up from 399,000 in 123 counties this year. More than a million people are losing standalone Part D plans, whose deductible rises from $615 to $700.

**In plain terms:** Medicare Advantage is the privately run version of the US government health plan for people over 65. Insurers get a fixed sum per person yearly and keep what they do not spend, so when local costs outrun that payment, exiting a county beats raising prices. The headline hides the problem. Total plan count barely changed, so on paper little happened, but the losses clustered, which is how 638,000 people end up with no private option.

**Why it matters:** Medicare Advantage was sold as a market that would discipline costs through competition, and the exits show the competition retreating from exactly the counties with the sickest and most expensive patients.

**Sources:** [Quartz](https://qz.com/medicare-open-enrollment-millions-new-plans-2027-101526) · [Straight Arrow News](https://san.com/cc/medicare-open-enrollment-begins-as-millions-face-fewer-plans-higher-costs/) · [PBS NewsHour](https://pbs.org/newshour/health/medicare-open-enrollment-starts-now-these-changes-will-arrive-in-2027)

## 5. WILDCARD - ECONOMICS

### South Korea's exchange halted trading as the global chip selloff deepened

The KOSPI fell 7.6% Thursday to 5,554.22, steep enough to trigger a circuit breaker that halted trading, with SK Hynix down more than 10% and Samsung Electronics 8.73%; the index has lost over 16% in a month. CNBC reported the 20 most valuable global AI and semiconductor stocks shed more than $1 trillion in market value over the week, with concern centred on higher borrowing costs weighing on planned capital spending rather than on chip demand. Neocloud shares recovered after Nvidia's Jensen Huang said he saw no need to backstop CoreWeave.

**In plain terms:** A circuit breaker automatically stops trading when an index falls too fast, to interrupt panic selling. South Korea hit one because its market is concentrated in two memory-chip makers. What changed is not demand for chips but the cost of money to build the data centres holding them: long-term US rates near 5% make multi-year construction far dearer, and nearly all announced AI spending is borrowed. A neocloud buys chips on debt and rents them out, so it breaks first.

**Why it matters:** The selloff is repricing the financing of AI buildout rather than its demand, which hits the companies borrowing to buy chips long before it hits the ones selling them.

## 6. WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### Anthropic's investors are discussing a $1.5 trillion listing, down from $2 trillion

Reuters reported, citing three people who attended Anthropic's investor day in San Francisco on Wednesday, that some investors discussed a valuation around $1.5 trillion for the planned listing, against roughly $2 trillion previously expected. The reassessment follows third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, up about 45% from the prior quarter but a sharp deceleration from nearly 150% the quarter before. Investors are also weighing commitments to spend at least $518 billion over the next decade on cloud, computing and infrastructure. The figure reflects investor discussion, not a valuation Anthropic has set, and reporting on timing conflicts between November and 2027.

**In plain terms:** A valuation is what buyers collectively think a company is worth, and before a share listing it is a negotiation, not a fact. The number fell even though the business grew, which makes sense once you look at the rate: growing 45% after growing 150% tells investors the curve is bending, and a price this size is a bet on it continuing. The $518 billion is owed regardless. This comes from people in the room, not the company.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic is the first frontier lab to be priced by public-market investors, so the discount they apply to decelerating growth sets the reference point for every private AI valuation behind it.

## 7. WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### Medicare's agency is quietly reinstating people it cut from ACA plans, and will not say how many

KFF Health News reported Thursday that the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services has reinstated an unspecified number of people whose Affordable Care Act coverage it cancelled, according to insurance industry sources. On August 31, CMS cancelled 315,000 policies covering more than 760,000 individuals it said were unauthorized; a September rulemaking document said those enrollees signed up with broker help but lacked citizenship or immigration documentation, and issuers could find no claims or contact records. KFF found not all 760,000 were the "phantom" enrollees the government described. CMS will not say how many were restored: "Currently, there is no additional data to share," its press office said.

**In plain terms:** The Affordable Care Act marketplace is where people without job-based insurance buy it, often subsidised. This summer the government ruled 760,000 of those sign-ups fake and cancelled them at once. The quiet reinstatements admit the screen caught real people too. Part of the test was that the insurer had no record of a claim or a returned phone call, which is also true of a healthy person who bought insurance and has not needed it yet.

**Why it matters:** A fraud screen that cannot be audited cannot be corrected at scale, so the people wrongly dropped will mostly find out at a pharmacy counter rather than from a letter.

## 8. WILDCARD - INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### The IEA will push its remaining emergency oil reserves out faster

The International Energy Agency agreed Thursday to accelerate release of roughly 100 million barrels still outstanding from the 400-million-barrel emergency action its members announced in March, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel; about 330 million barrels have gone out so far. Spain said Thursday it would release 4.4 million barrels in October. The move follows pressure from President Trump, who has pressed European Union countries to draw down reserves or face a possible US diesel export ban, as he seeks lower prices before the November 3 midterm elections.

**In plain terms:** Rich oil-importing countries keep emergency stockpiles, coordinated through the International Energy Agency, for exactly this. The diesel share matters most: diesel moves freight, runs farm equipment and powers generators, so its price feeds into nearly every physical good, and refineries cannot make more without crude. Releasing reserves caps prices now at the cost of a thinner cushion later, which is why doing it before an election is part of the story. The stockpile is finite.

**Why it matters:** Spending down the diesel cushion to hold prices through an election leaves less room to absorb the next disruption in a waterway that is still being attacked.

## 9. TECH

### A second worker has died from the explosion at Intel's chip plant in Israel

Soroka Medical Center in Beersheba confirmed Thursday that a second worker injured in Tuesday's explosion at Intel's Kiryat Gat plant in southern Israel has died; a third remains in intensive care. Police suspect a gas cylinder malfunction during a transfer of material from delivery trucks into tanks, with one truck disconnected and another connecting when the blast occurred. Fire services said the fire involved nitrogen trifluoride, a toxic gas used to clean chipmaking tool chambers, though its role is unestablished. The police commander said he could point to no suspicion of criminal activity or negligence at this stage. The site houses Fab 28.

**In plain terms:** A fab is a semiconductor factory, and chips are only part of what is inside one. Making them needs a steady supply of aggressive industrial gases, and nitrogen trifluoride is among the harshest, used to strip residue off machines between steps. The blast happened at the least glamorous point in that chain, swapping a delivery truck onto a storage tank, where a pressurised cylinder's stored energy is most exposed. Police have specifically not alleged negligence.

**Why it matters:** The chemical supply chain feeding a fab is a safety surface that gets almost none of the scrutiny aimed at chip supply itself, and a single site can be the sole source of a specific process node.

## 10. TRUMP NEWS

### Trump cancelled his defense secretary's plan to livestream a military execution

Trump said on Truth Social on Thursday that the execution of Nidal Hasan, the Army psychiatrist convicted of the 2009 Fort Hood shooting, will proceed as scheduled on December 3 by firing squad but will not be streamed online, and that victims' families may attend in person. The post reverses a plan Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the week before. Vice President JD Vance said he would not watch, and House Speaker Mike Johnson warned footage could become a "recruiting tool for future terrorists." Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said Hegseth supports the decision and the two are "completely aligned."

**In plain terms:** A military execution runs under the separate justice system governing the armed forces, and the United States has not carried one out since the early 1960s. The plan was to broadcast this one. What makes the reversal notable is who objected: not opponents of the administration but the vice president and the Republican Speaker of the House, and their stated objection was practical, that footage would be useful to recruiters.

**Why it matters:** The objection that carried was tactical rather than ethical, which marks the limit of what this administration's own allies will accept as a message to broadcast.
