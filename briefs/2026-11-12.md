## 4. HEALTHCARE

### A regional health commission is deciding this week whether the US loses measles elimination status

The Pan American Health Organization convened a three-day closed-door review this week with the United States, Canada and Mexico to evaluate each country's measles elimination status; no classification had been announced for the US as of November 10. CDC data showed 5,182 confirmed cases in 2026 as of November 5, against 2,289 in all of 2025, across 43 separate outbreaks, with 96% of cases outbreak-linked. The CDC recognizes four measles deaths this year; state health departments have reported eight, across Pennsylvania, New York and Wisconsin. CDC director Erica Schwartz told Reuters the agency's response would not change if the status is lost.

**In plain terms:** A country is called measles-free when the virus stops spreading continuously inside its borders for more than twelve months. It can still arrive from abroad, but it dies out instead of taking hold. The US earned that label in 2000, and Canada lost its own in November 2025. Measles is among the most contagious diseases known and spreads wherever vaccination coverage falls below roughly 95%. Losing the status changes nothing doctors do; it is a scorecard entry. The number worth watching is the gap between the federal count of four deaths and the eight that states report.

**Why it matters:** The label is symbolic, but the vaccination gap that produced 5,182 cases is not, and it sets how large next year's outbreaks get.

Under the constitution no new election follows: President Prabowo Subianto must nominate two candidates within 14 days, and parliament must choose within 60. Gibran is the son of former president Joko Widodo, whose alliance with Prabowo the resignation may end. **In plain terms:** Indonesia is the world's fourth most populous country, with about 280 million people. Gibran became vice president in 2024 at the age of 37, after a court decision altered the minimum-age rule in a way that happened to make him eligible, while his father was still president. That shortcut is what came back to undo him, alongside disputes over his academic record. The constitution keeps the government running without a snap election, so the question is who Prabowo names next. Gibran was the link holding his alliance with Jokowi together.

**Why it matters:** Removing Jokowi's son from the ticket frees Prabowo from the arrangement that won him the presidency, and parliament, not voters, picks the successor.

## 8. WILDCARD - HEALTHCARE

### The FDA approved the first drug in a new class of blood thinners

The Food and Drug Administration approved Bayer's Sunharbo (asundexian) 50 mg tablets on November 9, the first Factor XIa inhibitor cleared in the United States. It is indicated to reduce the risk of a second stroke in adults after a recent ischemic stroke or high-risk transient ischemic attack. Over a median 14.2 months of treatment, the drug cut the risk of ischemic stroke by 26% compared with placebo. It is not approved for atrial fibrillation, where an earlier trial of the same drug failed.

**In plain terms:** Blood thinners stop the clots that cause strokes, but every existing one also makes dangerous bleeding more likely, so doctors are always trading one risk against the other. Factor XIa is a step in the clotting process that appears to matter more for harmful clots than for the ordinary clotting that stops a cut bleeding, so blocking it is meant to break that trade-off. The narrow label is the catch: the same drug failed in atrial fibrillation, a much larger market, so this approval covers only stroke survivors.

**Why it matters:** A clotting target that separates stroke prevention from bleeding risk would reopen anticoagulation for the elderly patients doctors currently leave untreated.

## 9. TECH

### A security firm found about 35,000 apps built with Grok Build open to anyone

The security firm Claroc reported that of 37,571 live apps built with Grok Build it tested, 35,578, about 94.7%, opened without any login, and that an estimated 650 to 800 held private or sensitive data including patient lab values, medical exam records, employee details and plaintext passwords. Claroc said 30 organizations, among them six Fortune 500 companies, had internal data sitting in open apps, and that it located the links through passive DNS records. Grok said on X that the report was incorrect because it describes a different feature. Coverage ran November 11 and 12.

**In plain terms:** Grok Build lets someone describe an app in plain language and have working software generated for them. What Claroc describes is not a break-in but a default setting: the generated apps were published at web addresses that anyone could open and that were easy to guess, so no password was ever required. The researchers found them by reading public domain-name records, which work like the internet's phone book. The company disputes the finding, saying the researchers examined a different product, and only one firm has published this, so treat the numbers as credible but unverified.

**Why it matters:** Tools that let non-engineers ship software move the security default from a deliberate decision to whatever the vendor happened to set.

### A petition forcing a House vote on a second Epstein files bill reached 218 signatures

A discharge petition filed by Representative Thomas Massie for the Epstein Files Transparency Act II reached the 218 signatures needed to force a floor vote on November 11, according to the House Clerk. Every House Democrat signed, along with four Republicans: Massie, Nancy Mace, Ralph Norman and Brian Fitzpatrick. Representative John Garamendi supplied the 218th. Republican leadership had argued the bill was unnecessary because the House Oversight Committee is already investigating. Under House rules the motion must sit for seven legislative days before a signer can call it up. A Reuters/Ipsos poll released November 9 put Trump's approval at 32%, its third consecutive reading at that level.

**In plain terms:** A discharge petition is the one procedure that lets rank-and-file members force a vote when their leadership refuses to schedule one. It needs 218 signatures, a majority of the House, which almost never happens, because signing means openly going around your own party's leaders. This is the second time it has worked on Epstein records: the first forced the original transparency act in 2025, which Trump then signed. Four Republicans were enough this time because every Democrat signed, so leadership's objection no longer controls whether the vote happens.

**Why it matters:** The vote is now set by House rule rather than by leadership, which removes the usual lever the White House uses to keep a bill off the floor.
