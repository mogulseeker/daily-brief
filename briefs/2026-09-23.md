## AI INDUSTRY

### China opens a data-security probe into DeepSeek and Moonshot over traffic sent to Claude

China's Cyberspace Administration has sent officials into the offices of DeepSeek and Moonshot AI to determine whether the two routed their own users' queries to Anthropic's Claude, according to The Information, whose September 22 report Bloomberg matched. The regulator first summoned all seven Chinese labs named in Anthropic's September 10 threat report, then narrowed to two. Anthropic alleged roughly 200 million Claude exchanges tied to unauthorized distillation, including more than 12 million attributable to DeepSeek over 14 days in July. Chinese AI shares fell: Z.AI dropped as much as 12% in Hong Kong, MiniMax 6.8%.

**In plain terms:** Distillation means training your model on another model's answers, which is cheaper than working the answers out yourself. Anthropic, the US company that makes the Claude chatbot, says two Chinese labs went further: when their own users asked a question, the question was quietly forwarded to Claude and Claude's answer came back wearing the Chinese company's name. The interesting part is who is investigating. Beijing is not acting to protect an American firm's property. It is asking whether Chinese users' data left the country.

**Why it matters:** The rule that finally constrains China's AI labs looks likely to be its own data-security law rather than American export controls.

## ECONOMICS

### Oil falls for a fifth day as Iran dangles a seven-day reopening of Hormuz

Brent crude fell to about $99 a barrel on September 22, a two-week low and a fifth straight session of losses, after a senior Iranian official told Japan's Kyodo News that Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington lifted its blockade of Iranian ports and halted operations in the strait. Traffic through the strait is down roughly 95%, from more than 100 ships a day to between five and 12, a disruption the International Energy Agency calls the largest in oil-market history. US gasoline averaged $4.48 a gallon on September 21, up 40.7% year over year.

**In plain terms:** Crude oil is about 57% of what you pay at the pump, so the strait and the gas station are the same story with a delay. Traders are not pricing whether Iran reopens the waterway; they are pricing the odds that it does. A 3% drop on a news report from one wire service is how thin that market has become. One caution: the seven-day offer comes from an unnamed Iranian official speaking to a single outlet, and Washington has not confirmed any of it.

**Why it matters:** Energy is why the Fed raised rates on September 16 for the first time since 2023, so a reopened strait would cut US inflation faster than anything the central bank has left to do.

## HEALTHCARE

### Roche's obesity drug cuts weight 15.5% in patients who also have type 2 diabetes

Roche reported on September 22 that enicepatide, an injected drug activating both the GLP-1 and GIP receptors, met both primary endpoints of a 48-week Phase 2 trial in adults with type 2 diabetes who were overweight or obese. At the highest dose, 24 mg, patients lost an average 15.5% of body weight with no visible plateau, and HbA1c fell 2.65 percentage points from a baseline of 8.1%. Ninety percent reached an HbA1c of 6.5% or lower and 62% reached the non-diabetic range. Discontinuations for side effects were 2.0% on drug against 0% on placebo.

**In plain terms:** HbA1c is a blood test that averages your blood sugar over about three months; 6.5% is the line for a diabetes diagnosis. People with type 2 diabetes normally lose noticeably less weight on this class of drug than people without it, so 15.5% in this group is the number that matters. Roche is late here: Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk already sell drugs that hit the same targets. After Roche's data in June, analysts at Jefferies called the drug largely undifferentiated. This is still a mid-stage trial, not the larger one regulators require.

**Why it matters:** A credible third entrant is the mechanism by which injectable obesity drug prices actually fall, and Roche just bought itself a seat at that table.

## WILDCARD - INTERNATIONAL AFFAIRS

### The US, Denmark and Greenland sign an Arctic security agreement with no expiry date

On the General Assembly sidelines on September 22, Trump signed a trilateral agreement with Denmark and Greenland granting the United States permanent basing rights on the island, authority to expand the Pituffik Space Base, and the power to bar any US adversary from establishing a base, military presence or sensitive investment there. Denmark and Greenland keep sovereignty and Greenland keeps its mining concessions. The accord has no end date, and if Greenland becomes independent it stays in NATO and assumes Denmark's obligations. It stops short of Trump's demand that the US acquire the island.

**In plain terms:** Greenland is a self-governing territory of Denmark, sits between North America and Russia, and hosts a US space and missile-warning base. Trump had spent two years saying the US should own it, which strained relations with a NATO ally. This deal gives Washington most of what ownership would have delivered in military terms, and nothing of what it would have delivered in legal terms. The detail that will matter later is the veto: Greenland cannot take a Chinese or Russian investment in anything sensitive without American consent.

**Why it matters:** Washington converted a territorial claim into a permanent veto, which is cheaper than annexation and harder for a future government in Nuuk or Copenhagen to unwind.

## WILDCARD - AI INDUSTRY

### California makes data centers pay for the grid capacity they consume

Governor Gavin Newsom signed seven bills on September 21 covering data-center electricity, water, land use and local oversight, the most detailed state rules in the country. Three address cost allocation: AB 2383, SB 886 and SB 1168 require operators to pay for the transmission and distribution upgrades their own load creates rather than spreading those costs across other ratepayers, and direct the Public Utilities Commission to set data-center rate structures. The thresholds determining which facilities are large enough to trigger the rules have not been written yet.

**In plain terms:** A large data center can draw as much electricity as a small city, and connecting it means new transmission lines and substations. Under the old arrangement, utilities spread that construction bill across everyone's monthly statement. These laws say the facility that created the demand pays for it. The catch is in the last sentence: the laws do not yet define how big a data center has to be before the rules apply. That number will be set later by regulators, and it decides whether this is strict or symbolic.

**Why it matters:** Every other state now has drafted language to copy, and the lobbying that matters has moved from whether AI pays for its own power to where the threshold sits.

## TECH

### Amazon blocks Meta's shopping agent while Shopify hands it a checkout

Amazon blocked Meta's Muse assistant from browsing and buying on its store on September 21, saying Meta never disclosed the agent would access Amazon, that Muse does not identify itself while browsing, and that it appears to capture and store customer credentials. The next day Shopify said it would let Muse complete purchases with Shop Pay across all Shopify stores; chief executive Tobi Lutke announced it on X. Shopify shares rose about 7% on September 22. Amazon has blocked agents from Google and OpenAI on similar grounds.

**In plain terms:** A shopping agent is software that clicks around a store on your behalf, comparing and buying without you reading the page. That is a problem for Amazon, which earns a large share of its profit selling ads against its own search results that an agent will never look at. It is an opportunity for Shopify, which makes money when a purchase completes and does not care who clicked the button. The same piece of software is a parasite to one company and a delivery truck to the other.

**Why it matters:** Whether agents become a normal way to shop will be settled by which retailers let them in, not by how good the agents get.

Central banks raise interest rates to slow price rises, which is something you do when inflation is a live problem. His own appointee did exactly that six days before the speech.

**Why it matters:** The rate decision, not the speech, is what sets borrowing costs for mortgages and business loans over the coming year.
