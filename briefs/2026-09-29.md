**Why it matters:** A frontier lab moving its own launch date sets a public benchmark that regulators and competitors can measure every other lab against — and it lands a day before Trump hosts AI chief executives at the White House.

## 3. ECONOMICS

### Iran's currency passes 2.5 million rials to the dollar

Traders in Tehran exchanged more than 2.5 million rials for one US dollar on Tuesday, a record set 27 days after the previous record of 2.2 million — roughly a 12% loss in dollar value in under four weeks. The slide has accelerated since Washington cut Iran's access to regional banks and blockaded its ports. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Fox News on Monday that Iran faced an economic "cataclysm."

**In plain terms:** The rial is Iran's currency, and the street rate is what Iranians actually pay for dollars. When a dollar costs more rials, everything imported — medicine, machine parts, grain — costs more, and rial savings are quietly worth less. Iran earns most of its foreign currency selling oil, so a naval blockade stops the tankers and stops the dollars arriving. The rate is the scoreboard, not the problem.

**Why it matters:** A collapsing currency cuts both ways in talks: it pressures Tehran to deal, and it shrinks what Washington can offer, because sanctions relief buys less the further the economy has already fallen.

## 4. HEALTHCARE

### Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 8,000 confirmed cases

The Democratic Republic of the Congo has recorded 8,067 confirmed Ebola cases and 3,901 deaths as of September 27, a case fatality rate of 48.4%. Cases span 63 health zones in seven provinces, concentrated in Ituri, where 28 of 36 zones are affected. The outbreak, declared a public health emergency of international concern on May 16, is caused by Bundibugyo ebolavirus, for which no vaccine or treatment is licensed.

**In plain terms:** Ebola is a family of viruses, and the vaccine and drugs the world spent a decade building all target one member of it, the Zaire strain. This outbreak is a different member, Bundibugyo, and the existing tools were never proven against it. That is why WHO authorized a trial in July to find out whether the Zaire vaccine protects against this strain at all, rather than simply deploying it.

**Why it matters:** The countermeasures that ended the 2018-2020 epidemic are strain-specific and unproven here, which leaves the response largely back to isolation and contact tracing.

## 5. WILDCARD - ECONOMICS

### The Trump administration finalizes weaker fuel economy rules for new cars

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration finalized rules on Monday requiring new car and light truck fleets to improve fuel economy by up to 1% a year, reaching a fleet average of 34.9 mpg in model year 2031, down from the Biden-era target of 50.4 mpg. The rule also ends credit trading between automakers for building electric vehicles. Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy said it would make vehicles more affordable.

**In plain terms:** These standards set an average across everything a carmaker sells, not a floor for any one model, so big trucks stay legal as long as efficient vehicles pull the average up. Credit trading let a company that missed its target buy compliance from one that beat it, which is how electric-vehicle makers earned money from rivals. Cutting the target and killing that market lowers the bar and removes a reason to build electric cars.

**Why it matters:** A fleet average 15.5 mpg lower in 2031 locks in higher US gasoline demand across the full 15-year life of every vehicle sold under the rule.

### AMD buys World Labs for $8.2 billion and hires Fei-Fei Li as chief scientist

AMD said on Monday it will acquire World Labs, founded by Stanford computer scientist Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock deal valued at $8.2 billion, with Li joining as executive vice president and chief scientist. It is AMD's second-largest acquisition after the roughly $50 billion Xilinx deal that closed in 2022. World Labs builds "world models" trained on video and three-dimensional data rather than text.

**In plain terms:** Chatbots learn from text, so they know what people have written about the world but have no working model of it. World models train on video and 3D scans instead, learning how objects move and occupy space — what robots and self-driving software need. Li built ImageNet, the image database that set off the modern deep-learning era in 2012. AMD sells chips, and is buying the people who decide what the next ones must be good at.

**Why it matters:** AMD is paying a language-model price for a field with almost no revenue yet, a bet that the next compute bottleneck is video and 3D rather than text.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier filed a 49-page motion for a temporary injunction on Monday in Highlands County Circuit Court against OpenAI and chief executive Sam Altman. **In plain terms:** A temporary injunction is a court order that freezes conduct while a lawsuit is still being argued, so Florida is not waiting for a verdict. This is one county's state court, and its orders would bind OpenAI's conduct rather than the technology, raising the question of whether a national product can be built to one state's rules. No state attorney general has previously asked a court to gate model development. The judge has not ruled.

**Why it matters:** If a judge grants even part of this, consumer protection law becomes the fastest route to regulating AI development in the US, well ahead of Congress.

## 9. TECH

### Starship reaches orbit for the first time and deploys 26 Starlink satellites

SpaceX's Starship reached orbit on its 14th test flight on Monday, launching from Starbase, Texas, and releasing 26 third-generation Starlink satellites — the first operational payload it has placed in orbit. One of the six Raptor engines on the upper stage shut down during ascent; SpaceX completed the orbit-insertion burn on the remaining engines and ended the mission at its first scheduled checkpoint, splashing down north of Hawaii about three hours after liftoff.

**In plain terms:** Every previous Starship flight flew a suborbital arc — a very tall throw — and never circled the Earth or released a working satellite. This one did both. Losing one of six engines on the way up and reaching orbit anyway is the part engineers care about, because it means the vehicle carries enough margin to absorb a failure. Cutting the flight short at a planned decision point was a choice about risk, not a malfunction.

**Why it matters:** Starlink's economics have been capped by how much mass a Falcon 9 lifts per launch, and the same capacity underpins SpaceX's contract for NASA's lunar lander.

He later told the AP the remark was taken out of context, saying there is "plenty of evidence in all of these cases."

**In plain terms:** A grand jury is a panel of citizens that hears a prosecutor's evidence in secret and decides whether to charge someone. Prosecutors control what that panel sees, which is why the profession's oldest joke is that a grand jury will indict a ham sandwich. This investigation targets Trump's political opponents, has charged no one, and both men he picked to run it have now left. One said on the record, before walking it back, that indicting without evidence is an ethical problem.

**Why it matters:** People familiar with the probe told the Daily Beast that Olsen was pressed to indict before the November 3 midterms — an anonymously sourced account the department has not answered — but what is on the record is that the constraint is the prosecutors themselves.
